## 1. Resolve the structural benchmark risk FIRST

### 1.1 Inspect and document the source interpretation

Read the original dataset publication (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dib.2026.113063; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13396958/), official Zenodo v4 archive (https://zenodo.org/records/20707207), README and generator, and the related 2024 OTF charging LNS (https://www.honda-ri.de/pubs/pdf/5818.pdf), plus the 2026 revisit (https://doi.org/10.1145/3779426). Cite exact sections or page numbers and separate explicit statements from inference. Search for any newer paper with the same LEC dataset or a solved reference. Do not equate an absence of results from a search with proof of novelty.

The release has two physical sites and each has one allowed visit. The publication describes virtual copies when allowed visits exceed one. Prompt 01 interprets this as a **fleet-wide** maximum. The starting SOC equals the required final reserve in the inspected cases. With positive traction consumption and no depot replenishment, every vehicle driving positive minutes must charge, hence under this interpretation at most two vehicles can operate positive-travel routes, even with 20 nominal vehicles. This is not by itself proof that a given instance is infeasible. Verify for all 20 files, including depots and any zero-distance cases. Check whether this is in fact the documented original intended constraint. Contact/author clarification may be needed; prepare a concise question draft (do not send without authorization). Do not silently reinterpret the visit allowance per vehicle or add station copies to the originals. If ambiguities remain, retain the conservative original-input contract, label it precisely, and treat alternate assumptions only as explicitly separate variants.

### 1.2 Quantify structural feasibility before expensive search

Create a per-instance table of N, K, initial battery, final reserve, battery capacity, number and allowed visits of stations, allowable active-vehicle upper bound (derive it from assumptions), maximum purchasable energy upper bound, and provable lower bounds on needed travel/energy or concurrent vehicles. For homogeneous vehicles starting at reserve, energy balance implies total travel energy <= total charge; each positive recharge is <= battery capacity, so total travel minutes <= sum of per-visit battery-capacity upper bounds / consumption. This loose necessary condition is useful, not a feasibility proof. Derive and unit-test any tighter bound (e.g. sum of minimum incoming arcs to compulsory service nodes, or provably mandatory simultaneous on-board intervals divided by capacity, or sum of shortest pickup-to-dropoff times / capacity using directed shortest-path lower bounds if metric assumptions fail). Respect zero arcs, station copies, time units, vehicle-specific parameters, depots, and numerical tolerances. Never claim infeasibility from a heuristic failure, timeout, or a nonrigorous lower bound. Save any certificate and derivation.

Attempt construction on development sizes, with multiple truly distinct randomized orders, and use bounded feasibility search if needed. Audit medium/large structurally by input-only bounds; do not tune on final instances. Classify cases as verified feasible (saved schedule), provably infeasible under stated contract (certificate), or unresolved. If conservative interpretation cannot support enough original cases, do not optimize on a modified instance and call it an official result. Consider a clearly labeled controlled extension of the original data with explicit extra station copies and visit counts, or a documented alternate interpretation only after evidence, alongside the unchanged originals. Report the difference and never pool them. Any additional profile/constraint regime must be predetermined based on physical/model rationale, not method outcomes. If the strict cohort is too narrow, the final verdict may be CONDITIONAL or NO-GO; do not force a positive conference claim.

If not resolved, state exactly that results apply to the explicit model contract, not undisputed published optimum values. Preserve source and original files; no BKS claims for this dataset.

## 3. Build a strong shared routing and charging foundation

Create modular `src/price_ride/optimization/` (or an equivalent clean architecture), extending the new repository only. Keep Prompt 01 comparator callable as a reference, not as the sole competitor. Separate passenger order (paired pickups/dropoffs and assigned depots) from explicit schedules and charging decisions. ### 3.1 Robust fixed-order scheduling and charging options

For a given passenger sequence, enumerate feasible empty-vehicle station insertion gaps and available station copies. Respect precedence, capacity, time windows, max ride, horizon, intermediate/final SOC and visit allowances. For single-charge fixed order compute minimum required kWh from energy balance, prefix/suffix energy, and detour travel; reject impossible amounts. Account for extra energy due to detour and actual charge time. Support multiple charging events when the contract permits and a single event cannot suffice; for small strict cases with two total visits, enumerate 0/1/2-event possibilities or use a documented bounded search. Never assert exactness when pruning or sampling. Keep feasible fallback. Distinguish no witness found and certified infeasible. Ensure unused vehicle routes are legal. Use a fixed-order time feasibility solver (difference constraints or LP for a fixed charging duration). Optimize service timing to minimize excess ride where possible, not just earliest schedule; if using sampled charge starts, include tariff and surplus boundaries, boundary-minus-duration, feasible interval endpoints and deterministic representatives. Check actual start/finish and interval overlap. Earliest schedule is not always least passenger-excess schedule. Do not claim continuous optimality unless proved; for small micro-instances cross-check with exhaustive grid or independent LP/MILP. Use a bounded option pool with reproducible dominance or cap rules; if an option can use different scarce station slots it is not safely dominated merely by local cost.

For the fleet, select compatible options globally under station-copy/visit limits and finite period surplus. For tiny two-visit cases exact enumeration of retained option combinations is sufficient; for larger variants use a small MILP/beam or bounded combinatorial selection with incumbent fallback. If using MILP, define selected option indicators and station-period demand from them; allocate local surplus once and remainder to grid with correct priority under the model contract. Verify aggregate energy allocation and chosen options with chronological replay. A restricted option-pool optimum is not a global routing optimum. Retain and log all timing/cost assumptions. Track solver wall-time, number of options, cache hits, pruned options and deadline failures. Favor lightweight algorithms; do not create an overengineered exact routing solver.

### 3.2 Common independent classical search backbone

Implement randomized regret/greedy pair insertion and bounded repair/backtracking for incomplete starts. Try legitimate route reassignments, paired relocation, paired exchange, and intra-route pair reinsertions; optionally related-request removal with regret repair. Ensure every request stays paired; never swap individual nodes without checking precedence. Include multiple starts and retain best complete feasible incumbent. Test the ability to open an unused vehicle only if station resources allow. Use an ILS or modest LNS with fixed shared operators, common perturbation/acceptance policy, fixed budgets and a stopping criterion. Build both a price-blind control and a full-objective control; both must have competent energy-feasible charging repair. Do not handicap baseline search by hiding obvious feasible charging options or giving unequal compute. Keep traceability of candidate and accepted moves.

## 4. PRICE-RIDE contribution: marginal charging-opportunity-guided paired moves

The targeted novelty hypothesis is not generic LNS or time-aware charging insertion (both well-established, see Bresich et al.). It is that exploiting cheap charging opportunities during **passenger-request reassignment and ordering** finds joint improvements beyond an equally competent route-and-charge method. Start with a small, interpretable mechanism, not RL, deep learning, multi-agent systems or generic adaptive weighting.

Define objective J = travel_eur + excess_ride_eur + energy_eur. For a feasible incumbent S, form a per-route or per-gap opportunity descriptor: station availability, feasible empty-vehicle charging start interval or sampled interval, needed energy, candidate station detour and incremental passenger excess, marginal grid/LEC price exposure with residual surplus. Estimate prospective net value from change in travel/user cost plus change in energy bill (not energy-only ranking). Use actual price profiles, including finite LEC surplus; a cheap approximation is a ranking proxy, not a certified bound unless proved. Re-evaluate selected moves exactly under the shared evaluator. Crucially, a cheaper session that adds travel cost larger than energy savings is not a positive objective improvement. From 20 ToU pilot, EUR 2.44 energy savings buys only about 6.8 extra travel minutes at EUR 0.36/min, before excess-ride cost.

Design one central operator: **charging-opportunity-guided paired reassignment**. Select an expensive/limited charging route, identify a request or small paired block whose removal or relocation may free a relevant empty-vehicle gap, decrease required energy/detour, or shift feasible charging into a cheaper interval. Consider insertion on other routes or reordering on the same route, including joint exchange between two routes. Use a cheap incremental estimate to shortlist candidates; compare a price-aware score and a price-blind counterpart based on the same features minus time-varying price/LEC benefit. Reoptimize affected routes' charging options and globally reconcile station resource allocation. Include a controlled low-cost exploration mechanism (random/related-request moves) so the algorithm does not get stuck on currently expensive sessions. Use a fallback to the shared classical operators. Log how frequently the price-aware ranking actually changes chosen moves, whether predicted improvement is realized, and the decomposition of each accepted delta. If prices are constant and LEC discounts absent, price-specific guidance should not create arbitrary advantages; test a controlled flat/no-discount microcase. Do not optimize artificial tariffs to maximize the effect.

Write pseudocode, mathematical scoring definitions, complexity, and clearly state approximations. Tune at most a small number of parameters on the development panel; include exact tried settings in logs. Do not build a large new method if the opportunity audit finds no evidence of net benefit.

## 6. Go/no-go decision (predeclare before comparison)

A conference paper about PRICE-RIDE needs evidence of a **joint price-guided benefit**, not just generic routing gains. Freeze a practical development threshold before outcomes: e.g. median paired total-cost improvement >=0.5% relative to B3 on complete common-feasible cases, positive direction on at least half of distinct feasible development configurations, no systematic feasibility loss, and mechanism traces demonstrating genuine price-induced choices. These are planning thresholds, not universal publication criteria. A smaller benefit can be scientifically relevant if robust and offset by substantial charging-cost or compute savings without service deterioration; label this conditional and explain. Do not lower thresholds after viewing results. Also require a clear independent primary benchmark cohort with enough valid cases (propose at least four distinct original size/tariff configurations with more than one request size, plus a credible route to larger evaluation), reliable replay, and no unresolved material objective bugs. A tiny deterministic panel is not evidence of generalization. If there is little price-guided signal, but B3 is better than B1, report generic routing benefit rather than credit PRICE-RIDE. If station constraints make the official cases unsuitable, report the structural problem and recommend explicit alternatives, not a concealed modification. If all joint moves worsen full objective, classify NO-GO for superiority and stop costly method expansion. No forced success.

Verdicts: **GO** for Prompt 03 only when foundation correct, cohort defensible, and mechanism supports a credible comparative study; **CONDITIONAL** when meaningful progress but unresolved model/feasibility/novelty or weak signal; **NO-GO** when method lacks headroom or dataset is incompatible. A conditional result requires specific evidence and a realistic next step. A negative result is acceptable.
